04

Your website, as AI reads it

The site has a solid technical base. What it lacks is pages that answer the questions people ask about towns beyond Greenfield.

Strengths

  • Business details marked up for search engines: address, phone, hours, email
  • Clear Residential, Commercial and Irrigation pages
  • More than 40 years in the Pioneer Valley, stated on the homepage

Gaps

  • The 36 towns you serve are a list of map links, with no page saying what you do in Northampton, Amherst or Hadley
  • Your name appears two ways: “Snow & Sons Landscaping” on the site and Google, and “Snow and Sons, Tree and Landscaping” in the site’s business markup
  • The Our Services page is four headings with no description
  • The homepage has no meta description, and its main heading is a slogan rather than what you do and where
05

What would move the needle

Three fixes we’d make, and two you can do this week on your own.

1
Show up where you already work

Town pages for your Hampshire County service area

Start with Northampton, Amherst and Hadley: what you do there, for whom, with project photos. It gives AI assistants something to cite when someone asks about those towns.

2
Answer-first content

Rewrite the services page and homepage around real questions

“Who handles commercial landscaping for HOAs in Franklin County?” answered on the page, in plain language, and a homepage heading that says what you do and where.

3
One identity everywhere

Same name, services and service area on your site, Google and directories

One business name across the site markup, Google profile and listings, plus expanded Google profile services and service areas, and cleanup of directory listings showing low or outdated ratings.

You can do this today

Ask your next ten Hampshire County customers for a Google review, and suggest they mention their town and the work you did.

A five-minute fix

The phone number in your site header links to “rel:” instead of “tel:”, so tapping it on a phone may not start a call.
